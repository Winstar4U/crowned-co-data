# Shopify Collective as a Sourcing Channel for Bridal/Wedding Products (Luzilly Bridal & Apparel)

## What is Shopify Collective, mechanically, and how does the supplier-to-retailer returns/refund flow work?

### Takeaway
Shopify Collective is a free, built-in Shopify app/sales-channel pairing (a "retailer app" and a "supplier app") that lets one Shopify store (retailer) import and sell another Shopify store's (supplier's) products without holding inventory; the supplier fulfills and ships directly to the end customer. Returns and refunds are governed by a **retailer-configurable returns policy inside the Collective app** — the retailer chooses who creates the return label, where the item is returned, the return window/fees, and whether a supplier refund auto-triggers a customer refund. Critically, **the retailer is always the one the customer sees and who is ultimately responsible for issuing the customer-facing refund**; the supplier separately reimburses/transfers funds to the retailer per their Collective payment agreement.

### Cited Findings
- "Shopify Collective lets retailers sell products from other Shopify stores without holding inventory. The supplier ships the order, while the retailer owns the customer relationship." — [ReturnZap: Shopify Collective Returns](https://returnzap.com/blog/shopify-collective-returns-with-returnzap)
- "Who refunds the customer on a Shopify Collective return? The retailer refunds the customer. The supplier reimburses the retailer separately according to the Collective agreement." — [ReturnZap: Shopify Collective Returns](https://returnzap.com/blog/shopify-collective-returns-with-returnzap)
- Shopify's own retailer-side help doc on returns policy rules, verbatim structure:
  - **Return processing** options: (1) "Collective requests that supplier creates return label" — customer return request auto-notifies the supplier by email, a return is auto-created in the supplier's Shopify admin, supplier uploads a label which is emailed to the customer; (2) "Your store provides return label" (customer returns to supplier, or to the retailer's own address — if returned to the retailer, Collective sync to the supplier is turned off and no supplier-refund rule applies); (3) "Collective takes no action" — retailer handles the return manually/outside Collective (legacy/manual flow). — [help.shopify.com: Returns policies in the Shopify Collective app](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/retailers/policies/returns)
  - **Return window and fees**: retailer sets a preferred return window (Shopify's stated default is **30 days**), with options for a return label fee and/or restocking fee charged to the retailer by the supplier. — [help.shopify.com: Returns policies](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/retailers/policies/returns); [help.shopify.com: Suppliers — Policies](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/suppliers/policies)
  - **Supplier refunds** rule, two options: (1) "Automatically refund customer" — "When a supplier sends a refund amount to you, a refund is also issued to your customer automatically through the Shopify Collective app"; (2) "Collective takes no action" — "When a supplier refunds an item in their Shopify admin, the customer is then refunded automatically through any refund trigger that you have in a third-party returns app, or you can issue a refund to your customer manually through your Shopify admin." — [help.shopify.com: Returns policies in the Shopify Collective app](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/retailers/policies/returns)
  - Partial returns: "When a return contains more than one item, and the supplier refunds all the items, the return in your Shopify admin is automatically closed. If the supplier doesn't refund all the items ... you need to manually refund the customer for the item or items that are accepted for return." — [help.shopify.com: Returns policies](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/retailers/policies/returns)
- Supplier-side mechanics: "Returns are automatically created in your Shopify admin. Return labels and return reasons are synced to your Shopify admin. When you close a return, this syncs to the retailer's store." And on refunds: "You need to agree with your retailer how you handle refunds for Shopify Collective orders... When both you and your retailer have activated automatic payments on Collective, the refund is automatically transferred to the retailer after you initiate the refund request. You can refund Shopify Collective orders the same way as you refund your other orders in your Shopify admin." — [help.shopify.com: Fulfilling orders and managing returns on the Shopify Collective sales channel (suppliers)](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/suppliers/fulfillment)
- Developer-facing mechanics (shopify.dev), describing the underlying webhook/API flow: "1. The retailer chooses how to handle returns. 2. Customers initiate a return on the retailer store (via the Returns app or other 3P apps). 3. The return is routed to the supplier or the retailer depending on the retailer's choice. 4. The supplier or the retailer processes the return." Collective creates a return on the supplier's order upon receiving the `returns/approve` webhook; after the supplier processes/refunds, Collective restocks and closes the retailer-side return and notifies via `reverse_fulfillment_orders/dispose` and `returns/close` webhooks. If "Automatically refund the customer" is selected, "Collective processes the refund for returned items," and retailers are notified via the `refunds/create` webhook — "this streamlines the refund process ... but limits the types of refund policies that can be offered." Under "No Action," Collective does not create a supplier-side return at all, and the retailer must manually process the refund/restock via the `returnProcess` and `returnClose` GraphQL Admin mutations. — [shopify.dev: Handle returns (Collective app docs)](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/collective/returns)
- Retailer order-management doc confirms the payout linkage again: "If both you and your supplier have activated automatic payments on Collective, then the funds are transferred to you automatically after the supplier initiates the refund process." — [help.shopify.com: Managing orders in the Shopify Collective app](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/retailers/managing-collective-orders)
- Community guidelines place an explicit obligation on both sides: retailers must "ensure that customer returns are genuine and that they're made within the agreed returns window," and suppliers must "follow the returns policy that you agree to with your connected retailers." — [help.shopify.com: Collective Community Guidelines (Retailers)](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/retailers/community-guidelines); [help.shopify.com: Collective Community Guidelines (Suppliers)](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/suppliers/community-guidelines)
- A real-world complication reported on Shopify's own community forum: Collective's automated return flow only accounts for return-label-fee deductions, not for recovering the retailer's original outbound shipping cost when a customer keeps the item or doesn't ship a physical return; several forum posters describe having to manually adjust/partial-refund and reconcile supplier payouts outside the automated flow. This is community discussion, not official documentation, but corroborates that the "automatic" refund flow has real gaps retailers have had to work around manually. — [Shopify Community: Refund/Return on Shopify Collective](https://community.shopify.com/t/refund-return-on-shopify-collective/630672)
- Mechanically, on Shopify's own Collective landing page, the core retailer/supplier loop is summarized as: retailer "Discover suppliers ... Curate from supplier catalogs in seconds" → "At checkout, Collective routes orders directly to your suppliers to fulfill and ship direct to customers. No stock to handle, no hassles" → retailer "Track orders centrally in your Shopify admin, and only pay suppliers for the products you sold once they've shipped them." And under automations: "**Seamless returns**: Retailers set clear rules for returns and refunds processing, whether using a 3rd-party app or via the Shopify admin." — [shopify.com/collective](https://www.shopify.com/collective)

### Inferences
- Luzilly, as a retailer with **no warehouse and no capacity to receive/process returned merchandise**, would need to configure its Collective returns policy to **"Collective requests that supplier creates return label"** (so returns are routed to and handled by the supplier, never to Luzilly's own address) and set **"Automatically refund customer"** for supplier refunds, so Shopify Collective auto-issues the customer refund once the supplier processes their end — minimizing manual intervention. The alternative "Your store provides return label... Customer returns products to: Your address" option is explicitly unsuitable for Luzilly since it requires a physical inventory location to receive returns.
- Even with full automation selected, Luzilly would still be the **merchant of record for the refund to the customer** (the funds move customer ← Shopify Collective/retailer ← supplier), so Luzilly bears first-line customer-service and dispute exposure (e.g., a customer disputing a credit-card charge) even though the physical return goes to the supplier.
- The forum-reported shipping-cost reconciliation gap suggests Luzilly should not assume 100% hands-off automation in practice — partial refunds, shipping-cost clawbacks, and supplier payout reconciliation may still require manual review even under the "automatic" settings.

### Gaps
- No official Shopify documentation was found stating a firm SLA (e.g., "suppliers must refund within X business days") for how quickly a supplier must act once a return is received — the mechanics describe the *trigger* that fires a customer refund, but not an enforced turnaround-time guarantee.

## Does Shopify Collective have a published, platform-level minimum return/refund policy or window, or is it left to each supplier?

### Takeaway
There is a published **default** policy (30-day return window, supplier creates the label, no label/restocking fee by default, automatic customer refund) documented by Shopify, but the platform explicitly treats this as a **retailer-configurable starting point, not a mandatory floor** — the retailer can change the window, choose "Collective takes no action" (i.e., opt out of any automated return/refund handling entirely), and the return window is described only as the retailer's "preferred" setting. Shopify itself says in its own materials that "Collective can coordinate the return workflow, but it does not replace commercial agreement between partners" — i.e., there is no platform-enforced minimum that binds suppliers regardless of what the retailer configures or what the retailer and supplier separately agree.

### Cited Findings
- Shopify's help documentation states the defaults plainly: "## Default returns policies - **Return processing**: Supplier creates return label, 2-day processing time - **Return window and fees**: 30 days, no label fee, no restocking fee - **Declined and canceled returns**: Automatically cancel return - **Supplier refunds**: Automatically refund customer" — [help.shopify.com: Returns policies in the Shopify Collective app](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/retailers/policies/returns)
- The return window is framed as a retailer setting, not a locked platform rule: "Return window for Collective products | Set a preferred customer return window for Collective products. For example, you can set a 30-day return window." — [help.shopify.com: Returns policies in the Shopify Collective app](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/retailers/policies/returns)
- A third-party summary corroborates that this is a default, not a floor: "Shopify's current default policy has the supplier create the return label, uses a 30-day return window, applies no default label or restocking fee, and can automatically refund the customer after the supplier refund." — [ShopX Commerce: What Is Shopify Collective?](https://shopxcommerce.com/blogs/all/shopify-collective-explained-how-it-works-who-should-use-it)
- The platform explicitly disclaims enforcing alignment between parties: "**What Happens When Return Policies Do Not Match?** Shopify Collective can coordinate the return workflow, but it does not replace commercial agreement between partners." — [ShopX Commerce: What Is Shopify Collective?](https://shopxcommerce.com/blogs/all/shopify-collective-explained-how-it-works-who-should-use-it)
- The retailer can select "Collective takes no action" for return processing, in which case "all remaining rules are removed from your returns policy and they don't apply to your store" — i.e., there is no fallback enforced minimum once a retailer opts out; the retailer must handle it via "legacy manual methods outside of Shopify Collective." — [help.shopify.com: Returns policies in the Shopify Collective app](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/retailers/policies/returns)
- A real-world example of a retailer setting a *longer-than-default* window specifically for Collective products: David's Bridal's published Vendor/DTC manual states "For traditional DTC vendors, the return window is fourteen (14) days of receipt of merchandise. For Shopify Collective Vendors, the return window is thirty (30) days of receipt of merchandise. Customers are refunded once they ship their return and may cancel a pending return at any time until the return ship[s]." — [David's Bridal: DTC Manual](https://www.davidsbridal.com/legal/dtc-manual) (primary source, retailer's own legal/vendor-facing documentation; confirms the 30-day default is what at least one major bridal-adjacent retailer actually uses for its Collective vendor channel, without lengthening or shortening it)

### Inferences
- There is no platform-mandated minimum return window that binds a supplier regardless of the retailer's configuration or bilateral agreement — the "30 days / supplier-label / auto-refund" settings are Shopify's **default template values**, which the retailer (Luzilly) can change, and the supplier can presumably decline to connect with a retailer whose policy they find unacceptable (connection is opt-in on both sides).
- This means Luzilly cannot rely on "Shopify Collective's policy" alone as a guarantee that a bridal supplier will honor any particular return window or refund speed — the real governing terms are whatever return policy Luzilly configures in its own Collective retailer settings, applied uniformly across however many suppliers it connects to, *and* whatever the supplier is willing to accept for their own "Return window and fees" / restocking-fee rules on their end (both sides have policy settings, and the suppliers' policies are shown to Luzilly before connecting).

### Gaps
- No Shopify documentation was found specifying what happens, procedurally, if a retailer's configured return window/policy and a supplier's own configured policy actively conflict (e.g., retailer wants 30 days, supplier only offers 14) — beyond the general statement that Collective "does not replace commercial agreement between partners." It is unclear whether Collective blocks a connection in that case or simply applies the retailer's return-acceptance rule while leaving reconciliation of fees/label costs to manual negotiation.

## Which real, currently-active bridal/wedding/formalwear brands are genuinely connected to and selling through Shopify Collective?

A real, substantive search effort found a small number of **CONFIRMED** bridal/wedding-adjacent participants in Shopify Collective — most notably **Dare and Dazzle** (a wedding-gown rental/bespoke-bridal brand with a live, self-hosted Shopify Collective supplier-connect page) and **Lara New York** (an evening/mother-of-the-bride dress label, confirmed as a Collective-tagged vendor by a live retailer collection and by a cross-channel eBay listing). Shopify's own general marketing page for Collective does **not** feature any bridal/wedding brand among its named example brands (Fear of God, Beast, True Classic, Humanrace, Newton, Branch, Porter Road, Bala, Kyte Baby, Blendjet, Kitsch, Cozy Earth, Ten Thousand, Larroudé, Andie, Criquet, Funboy — all non-bridal), and Shopify's own wedding-industry blog content ("Trending Wedding Products to Sell Online") that name-drops Collective as a sourcing tool for the wedding niche discusses only generic decor/favor items (paper invitations, proposal boxes, dance flooring, disco balls) with **no named bridal apparel brand** at all. David's Bridal — the large bridal retail chain — runs its own "Shopify Collective Vendors" program as a **retailer**, per its own legal Vendor/DTC manuals, which is strong evidence of Collective's use at scale in the bridal retail category, but this documents David's Bridal as a retailer/host of Collective vendors (for its own storefront), not a confirmation of which specific gown or accessory brands supply it.

### Inferences
- The clearest, most defensible fit for Luzilly among everything found is **Dare and Dazzle** (wedding gowns, self-confirmed Collective supplier) and, for mother-of-the-bride/evening formalwear specifically, **Lara New York** (cross-confirmed via an independent retailer's live filter facet and an independent eBay listing). Both would need direct outreach/connection-request verification by Luzilly before any commercial commitment — this research confirms *participation*, not current product availability, live pricing, or willingness to connect with a new retailer.
- The near-total absence of bridal-gown/bridesmaid brands in Shopify's own promotional materials (landing page logos, named case studies, and its own wedding-sourcing blog post) suggests bridal apparel is not a flagship or heavily marketed vertical within Shopify Collective — the category appears to exist via organic brand-to-brand adoption (individual bridal brands installing the free supplier app) rather than via any curated, Shopify-promoted "wedding/bridal" marketplace section.
- Given the thinness of confirmed bridal-apparel-specific (as opposed to MOB/evening or accessories) supply, Luzilly should treat Shopify Collective as **a mechanism it could use if and when it finds/connects with a willing bridal supplier**, not as a channel with an established, discoverable roster of bridal-gown brands ready to connect today.
