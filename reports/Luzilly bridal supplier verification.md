# No supplier yet clears Luzilly's bar

Across 20 researched bridal/wedding suppliers — the 9 originally named candidates, two Shopify-Collective-confirmed brands, and nine additional worldwide leads — **not one combines verified supplier-direct fulfillment, genuinely explicit wholesale/dropship return terms, and buyer's-remorse coverage that returns cash rather than store credit**, the precise combination Luzilly Bridal & Apparel needs as a warehouse-free, returns-free-of-owner-handling Shopify retailer. Three suppliers (**AW Bridal**, **UCALLA**, **BrydealoFactory**) are workable-but-imperfect (YELLOW): their dropship mechanics are sound, but either the wholesale-specific return terms were never separately published (AW Bridal, UCALLA) or they exist and are explicitly restrictive — store credit only, plus a 35% restocking fee and all freight charged back to the buyer (BrydealoFactory). A second tier of nine suppliers, including both confirmed Shopify Collective participants (**Dare and Dazzle**, **Lara New York**), sits at UNKNOWN because pricing or return policy is gated behind a login or a private connection request that was never accessed. The remaining eight — most strikingly **KissBridal** and **SMC Fashion**, which are otherwise the two best-documented, most professionally run candidates in the entire set — are RED precisely because each one's own wholesale/dropship-specific policy states **final sale, no returns, no refunds** outside of a narrow defect window, which directly conflicts with Luzilly's requirement to cover buyer's-remorse, fit, and change-of-mind returns. **Everly Bridals has permanently closed** and should be dropped from consideration outright. The headline finding for the approval packet: Luzilly cannot yet greenlight a supplier on documented wholesale terms alone — every candidate in the YELLOW-or-better range still needs direct written confirmation of restocking fees, return-shipping responsibility, and refund destination before onboarding.

## Section 3 — Shopify Collective findings

**How the mechanism actually works.** Shopify Collective is a free retailer/supplier app pairing that lets a Shopify store import and sell another Shopify store's products with the supplier shipping direct to the end customer and no inventory held by the retailer ([ReturnZap](https://returnzap.com/blog/shopify-collective-returns-with-returnzap)). The return mechanics are **retailer-configurable, not platform-mandated**: Shopify's own default template is a 30-day window, the supplier creates the return label, no label/restocking fee by default, and the customer is refunded automatically once the supplier initiates its own refund — but Shopify's documentation is explicit that this default is a **"preferred" starting setting, not a floor**, and that "Collective can coordinate the return workflow, but it does not replace commercial agreement between partners" ([help.shopify.com](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shopify-collective/retailers/policies/returns); [ShopX Commerce](https://shopxcommerce.com/blogs/all/shopify-collective-explained-how-it-works-who-should-use-it)). Critically, **the retailer — Luzilly — remains the merchant of record who refunds the customer**; the supplier separately reimburses the retailer per their private Collective agreement, which is itself where most of the real exposure sits, since the automated "customer refund" trigger only fires once the supplier processes its own end, and forum reports describe retailers having to manually reconcile shipping-cost clawbacks outside the "automatic" flow ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/refund-return-on-shopify-collective/630672)). For Luzilly specifically, the correct configuration would be "Collective requests that supplier creates return label" (never the retailer's own address) paired with "Automatically refund customer" — but even then, Luzilly carries first-line customer-service and chargeback exposure until the supplier's refund actually posts.

**Confirmed currently connected.** Two bridal/wedding-adjacent brands were independently confirmed as live Collective suppliers: **Dare and Dazzle** (wedding gown rentals and bespoke bridal designs), which runs its own first-party "We use Shopify Collective to partner with retailers" connect page ([dareanddazzle.com](https://dareanddazzle.com/apps/collective/retailer-referral)); and **Lara New York** (mother-of-the-bride/evening, not bridal gowns), cross-confirmed by an independent retailer's live, matching vendor-and-"Shopify Collective"-filter product counts and by an eBay listing explicitly tagging both "Lara New York" and "Shopify Collective" ([SARAH FASHION](https://sarahfashionny.com/collections/mother-of-the-bride?filter.v.option.color=Green&page=2); [eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/407206018193)). Neither brand's Collective-specific return terms or pricing were locatable — both are gated behind a private retailer connection, so both remain UNKNOWN as *onboardable* suppliers despite being CONFIRMED as *participants*. Separately, **David's Bridal** — a major national bridal chain — confirmed it runs a parallel "Shopify Collective Vendors" track for its own vendor relationships, per its own published DTC and Vendor Terms manuals, including the detail that for Collective vendors "DBI Customer Service handles payment and generation of return shipping labels directly with the vendor" ([David's Bridal DTC Manual](https://www.davidsbridal.com/legal/dtc-manual)). This confirms Collective is used at real scale in bridal retail, but David's Bridal is a *retailer* using Collective, not a brand Luzilly could source from, and the specific brands supplying David's Bridal via Collective were not named anywhere found.

**Unverified possible.** **Iyza Belaar** appears alongside a "Shopify Collective" filter facet on one retailer's site, but its own Instagram describes a parallel traditional-wholesale channel with a **stated 10-piece MOQ** — directly in tension with Luzilly's no-bulk-buy requirement if that channel, not Collective, is what governs ([bchicfashions.com](https://bchicfashions.com/collections/iyza-belaar); Instagram). **A & M Enterprises Bridal** showed a "Shopify Collective" tag in indexed structured data on its accessories collection, but a live re-scrape could not confirm which specific vendor on that page is Collective-sourced — and in any case A & M is itself a *retailer*, not a supplier Luzilly could connect to. Per the task's explicit instruction, neither is upgraded to CONFIRMED.

**Not found.** Shopify's own general Collective marketing page names eleven example brands and three case studies — **none bridal** — and Shopify's own wedding-industry blog post recommending Collective as a sourcing tool for wedding products names **no bridal apparel brand at all**, listing only decor/favor categories ([shopify.com/collective](https://www.shopify.com/collective); [Shopify wedding blog](https://www.shopify.com/blog/wedding-trending-products)). No press release or brand announcement of a bridal-gown or bridesmaid label joining Collective was found. **Conclusion for the packet: Shopify Collective is a real, usable mechanism, but bridal apparel is not a curated or Shopify-promoted vertical within it — any connection would be organic, brand-by-brand, and would need direct outreach to Dare and Dazzle (the strongest fit) to even begin verifying returns/cost terms.**

## Section 5 — Worst-case return/cash-exposure flags

For the three YELLOW suppliers — the only ones where onboarding is plausible pending further verification:

- **AW Bridal:** Because wholesale/dropship-specific return terms were never published separately, the worst case is that the published consumer policy (30 days, cash refund, buyer's remorse allowed) **simply does not apply to a dropship account at all**, leaving Luzilly to self-fund any refund with no confirmed recourse against the supplier — compounded by the documented discrepancy between the policy's stated "US warehouse" return point and at least one customer's reported China return address with high shipping cost.
- **UCALLA:** The underlying terms (30-day window, buyer's remorse accepted, cash refund to original payment) are workable in principle and do not require Luzilly to hold inventory, but because the restocking fee and who-pays-return-shipping are both undocumented, Luzilly cannot currently quote a customer accurate return costs — and a gown shipped back to Suzhou, China for return could itself carry a large, unbudgeted shipping cost before any refund is confirmed.
- **BrydealoFactory:** The documented terms are explicit but structurally unfavorable: **store-credit-only plus a 35% restocking fee and all freight charged to the buyer** means a return never recovers cash for Luzilly or the customer, eroding margin on every single return — but it does not require Luzilly to receive or store the returned gown, since the item routes directly back to the Chinese factory.

No supplier currently rated GREEN exists, so there is no "ideal" exposure case to report; all three workable candidates still carry either an unquantified cost (AW Bridal, UCALLA) or a quantified-but-punitive one (BrydealoFactory).

## Conclusion

The research converges on an uncomfortable but useful finding for the approval packet: the suppliers with the most professional, best-documented wholesale programs — KissBridal and SMC Fashion — are disqualifying precisely *because* they are well-documented. Their clarity reveals final-sale terms that a vaguer, less transparent supplier might have let Luzilly assume away. The inverse is also true: UCALLA and BrydealoFactory look workable mainly because their terms are generous enough (or restrictive-but-survivable, in Brydealo's case) on the dimensions that were actually published, while critical fields — restocking fee, return-shipping payer, exact destination — remain open. That gap is the real decision point: Luzilly's onboarding sequence should not be "pick the top-ranked supplier," but "send the same four-question written request (restocking fee, shipping-cost responsibility, refund-vs-credit, and whether wholesale terms differ from consumer terms at all) to AW Bridal, UCALLA, and BrydealoFactory in parallel, and treat Dare and Dazzle's Shopify Collective connection as a worthwhile fourth track given it is the only option that structurally guarantees the supplier, not Luzilly, handles the physical return." Until those answers come back in writing, no supplier in this research clears the bar for a GREEN rating, and the business should budget for the likelihood that its first bridal-dropship relationship will carry real, uncapped cash exposure on at least some fraction of returns.
